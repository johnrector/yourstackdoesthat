The slogan of the era after apps

Your stack does that.

It means you have one intelligence. It is yours: direct from a frontier company, on your own bill, on your team and nobody else's. It has memory, so it never starts over. It has connectors into your records: your mail, your calendar, your money, your files. It has skills it learned once and can do again. And it has scheduled tasks, which run at five in the morning whether or not you remember them. Whatever the need, you do not go get a thing. Your stack learns it, connects to it, or schedules it.

It replaces "there's an app for that." That was a store's slogan. It taught a whole generation that the answer to any need was to go to a store, get a product, make an account, and eventually pay for it every month. The average American now pays about $111 a month across those charges. Most of them buy a single thin capability wrapped in a login. When the capability arrives inside the one system that already knows your calendar and your inbox, the standalone version stops making sense. Not because it got worse. Because it became a feature of something you already have.

What did you stop paying for?
