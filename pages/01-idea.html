From App to Stack

A different place
for your attention.

“Let your stack do that” is an invitation to hand over the work, not a prompt to buy another capability.

Apple’s 2009-era “There’s an app for that” campaign helped teach us to look for an application whenever a need appeared. We learned to move among apps and carry the relationships between them ourselves.

App People use software to achieve an outcome. Stack People delegate the outcome to an evolving personal AI. Both benefit from the result; the difference is how much work the person does along the way. Stack People give their AI enough context and authority to take responsibility. A personal database, a reasoning layer and a conversational layer form a continuing relationship. Tools and services provide capabilities within it.

The work leaves your attention

The assistant notices an obligation, understands it alongside the rest of your situation, acts within standing permissions and judges the outcome. You should not have to turn each receipt into the next prompt. An unsolicited draft leaves you a job; a thoughtful proposal sent to its recipient is progress you can rely on.

Batch the decisions

Our Design for Batch recommendation gives the executive one fresh holistic assessment on a timer. Discovery leads knowledge preparation, deep reasoning produces one plan, and finite jobs return receipts. The next batch treats those receipts as knowledge.

No long-running business workflow is needed for each request. Calls and texts remain responsive from permitted existing CRM knowledge; all substantive work is batched, without exceptions.

Let it learn

People and circumstances change. The assistant should administer its own CRM, discover new useful sources and revise its general practices from outcomes. Ordinary mistakes are opportunities to improve. The aim is a capable relationship that grows, not a collection of hardcoded rules for each person.

The result to judge is finished work and attention returned. Fewer subscriptions may follow. They are not the whole point.
