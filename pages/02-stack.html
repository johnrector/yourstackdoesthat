The personal stack

One relationship.
Room to grow.

App People use software to achieve outcomes. Stack People delegate the outcomes to a personal AI. Both benefit from the result. App People push the buttons along the way; Stack People let the AI carry the work.

Three central layers

A personal database
Relationships, preferences, commitments, evidence and actual outcomes. The person controls the stack and delegates ordinary CRM administration to the assistant. It can add fields, revise records and improve its organization without asking the person to become a database administrator.
A prior transcript can teach the assistant that a friend visited Rome. A later call can use that relationship fact naturally. New knowledge should survive an old schema mismatch.
A reasoning layer
One executive discovers what matters, prepares knowledge, understands the current situation and decides what work to do. Our recommendation is Design for Batch: a fresh holistic assessment on a timer, followed by finite assignments and receipts.
A conversational layer
Responsive calls and texts using only permitted CRM knowledge from completed batches. The assistant accepts new work for the next batch. Missing facts wait too; there is no new investigation during conversation. Email is entirely batched.

Proactive is part of the delegation

The assistant does not wait for you to prompt each job. Its scheduled batch discovers what changed and what needs doing, then takes responsibility within standing authority. You delegate an ongoing responsibility for outcomes, not merely a task every time you open a chat.

Tools serve the executive

Connectors supply authorized access to records. Skills describe ways to do things. Timers supply initiative and collectors refresh available knowledge. These are capabilities, not separate managers of the principal’s life.

The executive assigns purpose and consequential scope. Executors judge ordinary details and return evidence, including failure. Their receipts enter the next batch; they do not start an independent recovery strategy.

Finished work frees attention

An ordinary proposal should be prepared thoughtfully and sent under standing authority. A draft for the principal is not completion unless review was actually requested. At each batch, the assistant judges what happened and what needs doing now.

Each batch stands on its own. Relationships, commitments and receipts remain knowledge; the system does not need a long-running business workflow for every request. The next batch may change direction because the situation changed.

Build it, then judge it

Reliable wake-ups, account connections, persistent knowledge, deep reasoning and observable execution require setup. No single account automatically supplies the whole architecture. The Does list describes capabilities and limitations, not production certifications.

The stack stays yours. Its assistant should learn general practices from outcomes, revise old inferences and grow with your life. Ordinary imperfection should lead to improvement, not an ever-growing list of person-specific rules.
