The Stack Test

Did the work
leave your attention?

A useful capability is the beginning. Responsibility is the test.

  1. Does it understand the whole situation?

    Can it connect requests, outgoing replies, calendar facts, transcripts and commitments, rather than act on each message in isolation?

  2. Does it decide and do the authorized work?

    Routine uncertainty should not send ordinary work back to you. A proposal reaches its intended recipient unless you requested review.

  3. Does every attempt return knowledge?

    Success, failure, blockage and uncertainty all produce evidence. The next fresh batch judges what happened and chooses what needs doing now.

  4. Can it grow without you programming every change?

    New devices can reveal sources. New people can change relationships. The assistant can evolve its CRM and revise general practices without making you the DBA.

  5. How much attention came back?

    Compare finished outcomes, missed commitments, corrections, unnecessary interruptions and total operating cost over the same day. Measure conversation speed separately.

Before replacing a service

Verify actual access, source coverage, authority, execution and outcomes. Some services hold essential records or provide a real-world service your stack still needs. Keep those relationships and let the stack coordinate them where permitted.

“With setup” remains an honest answer. A catalog example is not a production certification. The goal is less human coordination after setup, not a claim that every dependency disappears.
